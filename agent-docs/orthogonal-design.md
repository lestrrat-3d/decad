# Orthogonal faceted solid design

Faceted solids whose every face squares to a world axis are read and rebuilt on a grid of their own coordinates, in `internal/orthoshell/`, so the results are exact. This document holds one part per operation. Each part keeps its own decision IDs and `§` numbers; cite a `§` number as "Part name §N".

## Shell

`Shell` of a faceted solid whose every face squares to a world axis, such as a part read from STL or STEP and made a solid by
`SolidFromMesh` (`agent-docs/mesh-solid-design.md`). The construction is plain-data grid code in `internal/orthoshell/`; the
root hook is `shell_orthogonal.go`, called from `Shell` before the receiver routes of `agent-docs/modify-design.md` §8.

### 1. What it builds

The wall of a shell of thickness `t` is the solid less its cavity. For a polyhedron whose faces all square to an axis the
cavity is the solid eroded by a cube of side `2t`, which is its inward offset with the offset planes meeting in a sharp corner at
every edge, the mitered offset a CAD shell makes. The walls are then a union of grid cells, and the result is read back as a mesh
solid. No Boolean runs.

| Decision | Rule |
|---|---|
| O0 | A solid two of whose vertex coordinates along an axis differ by a nonzero amount within the grid tolerance (a billionth of the larger of 1 mm and its extent) is refused, so the grid never moves a face by merging two lines; equal coordinates are one line. |
| O1 | The route applies to a faceted receiver whose held vertices all carry a zero bound, every triangle of which has its three corners on one world-axis plane exactly, in floats, inward, `Outward` or `Centered`. Any other receiver, and a receiver with a vertex bound, take the receiver's own route and its own refusal. |
| O2 | The coordinates of the grid are the solid's vertex coordinates on each axis, plus, for each removed face, its plane moved `2t` outward. Cells of that grid are occupied by the exact signed coverage of the x-facing triangles along each column, which must be 0 or 1 everywhere, and the grid's volume must equal the mesh's own; otherwise the solid is `ErrUnsupported`. |
| O3 | The solid joined to a slab `2t` thick over each removed face (the cells between its plane and `2t` out, over its footprint, the footprint summed over the face's triangles and taken where it exceeds half a cell) gives the occupancy `S⁺`. |
| O4 | The grid is refined by every coordinate `± t`. A refined cell is in the cavity when every cell of `S⁺` that the cube of half-side `t` about the cell's middle meets is occupied, which is constant over a refined cell because of the refinement. The cavity reaches `t` past a removed face, so it opens it. |
| O5 | The walls are the cells of the solid itself that are not in the cavity. Walls with no cells are `ErrDegenerate` (the cavity takes the solid), and a cavity with no cells is `ErrDegenerate` (the walls meet: `t` is not under half the thinnest part). |
| O6 | The wall mesh is the boundary of those cells, wound outward from the material, each cell face a quad fanned in four triangles from a point inside it that varies from quad to quad. The fan keeps a probe off the cell diagonals, where the evaluator's axis rays of later Booleans cannot classify it. A boundary that touches itself along an edge is `ErrUnsupported`. |
| O7 | `solidFromMeshBody` proves the walls a solid as `SolidFromMesh` does, and the result is committed as one `OperationShell` entry with the receiver consumed. Coplanar triangles group into faces by exact plane, and every face keeps its axis plane as a retained tag, so `Fillet` and `Chamfer` build on its edges. |
| O8 | `WithNoOpenings` builds the closed hollow body: an outer shell around the cavity's inner shell, with no slab. A selector removes faces as for any shell. |
| O9 | The offsets are float sums `x ± t`, so a wall is `t` thick to the rounding of that sum, and the volume the body reads is exact for the mesh built. Coordinates of the grid within `1e-9` of the body's size are one coordinate. |
| O10 | An `Outward` shell grows the walls off the solid, which becomes the cavity. The walls are the cells within a cube of half-side `t` of the solid, less the solid: the solid dilated by a cube, its planes offset and met in a sharp corner at every convex edge. At a removed face the wall ends flush with that face's plane: over the footprint grown by `t` nothing of the wall stands beyond the plane, and no slab joins the solid. `WithNoOpenings` gives the closed wrap. |
| O11 | A `Centered` shell puts half the thickness each side of the surface: the cells of the inward walls of thickness `t/2` joined to those of the outward walls of thickness `t/2`, both on the one refined grid the solid, the removed faces and `t/2` fix, so the union is a cell-wise OR (`orthoshell.CenteredWalls`). Over a removed face the wall is open in both halves. The closed form is a dilation less an erosion with an inner void, so it holds two shells. A receiver this route does not take is `ErrUnsupported` under `Centered`, since no other route builds that sense; walls whose inner halves meet are `ErrDegenerate`. |
| O12 | `WithShellFaceThickness(normal, t)` gives the walls whose outward normal is a world axis direction their own thickness, for an `Inward` shell. The cavity is the solid eroded by the box that reaches `t(-a)` toward `-a` and `t(+a)` toward `+a` on each axis `a`, computed on the refined grid whose lines are every coordinate plus and minus each thickness, so each wall is as thick as its direction says and walls of two directions meet in a mitred corner. A removed face's slab is as deep as that axis's two thicknesses together. A direction left unnamed takes Shell's thickness. A normal that is not exactly a world axis direction, a zero thickness, a direction named twice, a sense other than `Inward` and a cavity that lies only in the slab over an opening (the walls meet) are `ErrDegenerate`; a body that is not a faceted axis-square solid is `ErrUnsupported`. The tests give a block a 3 mm floor and a 4 mm east wall beside 1 mm walls and read the volumes, bounds and points. A thickness assigned to the opposite direction fails them. |

### 2. Not covered

- A receiver that is not orthogonal, and one with a vertex bound (a Boolean result, whose held vertices carry the evaluator's
  bound).
- A grid of more than four million cells.
- Walls whose boundary touches itself along an edge.
- Rounded inside corners: the offset is mitered, as the planes of a CAD shell are.

### 3. Tests

The internal tests work boxes and an L-shaped prism by hand: the walls' volume is the solid's less the cavity's, with the cavity
eroded from the floor to the open top, and the L's cavity has its sharp corner and area `5`. The public tests shell a box
and an L-shaped mesh solid, open and closed, and read points in the wall, the miter and the cavity.

## Boolean

`Union`, `Cut` and `Intersect` of two faceted solids every face of which squares to a world axis are computed on a
grid of the operands' coordinates, so they are exact and take any contact: a shared face, a coplanar overlap, a shared
edge. The grid code is that of the orthogonal shell (`agent-docs/orthogonal-design.md`) in
`internal/orthoshell/combine.go`; the root hook is `boolean_orthogonal.go`, called by `performBoolean` before the
general dispatch.

### 1. Contract

| Decision | Rule |
|---|---|
| B1 | The route applies when both operands are faceted bodies, every held vertex of a zero bound and every triangle exactly square to an axis, such as mesh solids (`SolidFromMesh`, `agent-docs/mesh-solid-design.md`), the results of `MoveFace`, `Stretch` and an orthogonal `Shell`, and the results of this route. Any other pair takes the general route. |
| B2 | Both solids are read onto one grid: the lines of each axis are the coordinates of the vertices of either operand that lie on two planes, an edge or a corner. A vertex on one plane is a point a mesh adds to fan a face and gives no line. The grid tolerance is a billionth of the larger of 1 mm and the pair's extent, and two distinct vertex coordinates within it, in one operand or between the two, are refused rather than merged, so no face of the result stands anywhere but on a coordinate it was given. |
| B3 | A cell is kept by the operation: `Union` where either operand occupies it, `Cut` where the first does and the second does not, `Intersect` where both do. The boundary of the kept cells is meshed as the shell meshes its walls, and `solidFromMeshBody` proves it a solid. The corners of every face of the result are coordinates of the operands, so the result is the exact common solid and carries no mesh bound. |
| B4 | The route only ever declines: a result with no cell, an unequal grid, a grid past `MaxCells`, kept cells that meet only along an edge (a non-manifold mesh) or a result the solid proof refuses leave the pair to the general route and its own error, so a disjoint `Intersect` is still `ErrBooleanFailed`. |
| B5 | The result replaces both operands as one `Union`, `Cut` or `Intersect` operation with the same input roles. |
| B6 | The tests join two blocks that touch at a face into one bar of six faces, take the union, common part and difference of a cube and a slab sharing a plane, cut a pocket whose floor and walls lie in the plate's planes, chain unions into a cut, and read volumes written out by hand with a zero bound, bounds, face counts and points. A union that keeps `||` as `&&` fails them. |
| B7 | Beside a faceted operand that meets B1, the other may be any solid whose tessellation (at 0.1 mm, verified) carries no displacement bound and has every triangle square to an axis, such as an extruded rectilinear profile: its mesh is the body exactly and is read as an operand. Two operands that are not faceted never take this route and keep their certified ones. |
| B8 | A result of this route is a mesh solid whose axis faces keep exact planes, so `Shell` (`agent-docs/orthogonal-design.md`) and `Fillet` and `Chamfer` of its straight edges (`agent-docs/retained-edge-blend-design.md`) take it, a convex and a reflex edge alike. The blend of a straight edge accepts the fan points the grid meshing leaves in a face, since they lie exactly on the face's plane and the face is built again from its loops. The tests fillet and chamfer the outer corner and fillet the reflex corner of an L-shaped union and read volumes from the section added or removed, and shell it open at the top. |
| B9 | A placed operand qualifies when its placement left every held vertex at a zero bound: a vertex whose exact image under the stored basis and translation is itself a float pays no rounding (`facetproof.PlaceMesh`), as a whole-number translation of whole-number coordinates does, and the held vertices are world coordinates, so the route reads them as they are. A rotation by an angle that leaves float images pays its rounding and so leaves the route. |
| B10 | `QuarterTurn(ctx, b, axis, about, turns)` turns a faceted solid by whole quarter turns about a world axis through `about` by exchanging and negating coordinates, which rotates exactly where a 90 degree `r3.Rotation` leaves vertex images that are not floats. Every relative coordinate `p − about` and every sum `about + turned` must be held exactly, proved by the error-free sum (a nonzero rounding error is `ErrUnsupported`), so the turned solid keeps a zero vertex bound and stays on the routes above. The tests turn a block about each axis, both ways, five turns, and about a point, read bounds and volumes, join a turned bar to another exactly, and refuse whole turns, a skew axis, a centre the sums round on, a non-finite centre, a non-faceted solid and nil arguments. A sum taken as exact when it rounds fails them. |
| B11 | A `Union` of three or more bodies, among them `PatternJoin`'s target and instances, tries the route after the certified prism, axial and copied-tooth routes refuse it: at least one operand is a faceted solid that meets B1 and the rest are that or meet B7, every part is read onto the one grid of all their coordinates (`orthoshell.UnionMany`), and the cells any occupies are kept. The result is built under the producer identity the call evaluates under. Two coordinates of different parts within the grid tolerance but unequal are refused, as in B2, and the set is then joined two at a time as before. A circular pattern's instances are rotated copies whose vertex images round, so they carry a bound and leave the route. The tests join three bars end to end, three with a prism in the middle, and a comb of nine teeth by `PatternJoin` with exact volume and points, and check that near-coincident faces leave the route. Disabling the hook fails the first and third. |
| B12 | `Verify`'s overlap volume of two faceted solids that each meet B1 is read on their common grid (`orthoshell.OverlapVolume`): the volumes of the cells both occupy are summed, with a bound above the sum's own rounding, so a pair sharing a face plane, which the mesh route refuses as contact, is measured, and a pair touching only along a face or an edge shares no cell and is not interference. The reading is tried after the analytic and prism readings and before the mesh route, and a coordinate near-coincidence refusal as in B2 leaves the pair to the mesh route. The tests overlap two blocks across shared y and z planes (200 mm^3), overlap two across an inner corner (84 mm^3), and read a U and a bar (120 mm^3, equal to the prisms' own reading). Disabling the hook fails all three. |
| B13 | A feature built from tool bodies, such as `Emboss`, `Hole`, `Boss` or a rib, combines its tool with the target through the unexported `booleanBody`, which tries the route of B1 to B8 first and keeps its certified routes when the route declines, so the result of such a feature on a faceted solid that meets B1 is exact. The public `Union`, `Cut` and `Intersect` try the route in `performBoolean` and do not repeat it. A fold of coplanar boxes needs no gap shift, so a test of a gap-shift warning uses a round operand to stay on the mesh route. The test raises and engraves a relief on a mesh plate with an `Exact` volume of 12120 and 11880 mm^3. Disabling the hook fails it. |
| B14 | Instance i of a `CircularPattern` of a faceted solid that holds no vertex bound is a whole number of quarter turns when `4i` is a multiple of the count (a count of 2 or 4) and the axis is exactly a world axis direction. Such an instance is built as `QuarterTurn` builds it (`quarterTurnVerts`), exact where a 90 degree rotation is not, and the instances join on the grid of B11. Any other count or axis takes the placed copy as before. `PatternCopies`, `Patterned` and `PatternJoin` share the instance builder. The tests join a pinwheel of four bars (1600 mm^3), four bars that overlap at the middle and two bars turned a half turn, each against a count of unit squares. Disabling the hook fails all three. |
| B15 | Every grid operation is checked against a cube by cube brute force on random solids (`internal/orthoshell/differential_test.go`): a few boxes of a 6 x 6 x 6 grid of unit cubes, with up to three cubes carved out again, meshed, run through `Combine` (union, cut, intersect), `OverlapVolume`, `Offset` both ways, `Walls` inward and outward `Undercuts` along each axis, `SectionLoops` at each layer, `HasConcaveEdge`, `MinWall` and `Press` of a planar face outward and inward, and compared with the same operation done cube by cube: the Boolean of two sets, the dilation and erosion by the cube of half-side one, a column test for each face, the cubes of each layer against the shoelace area of its loops, a scan of every grid edge for three solid cubes of four, the shortest run of solid cubes along any line of cubes for the thinnest wall, and the cubes just out from or just under a face region for a press by one cube. The meshes of the results are closed with every triangle square to an axis, and a shape the route declines (`ErrUnsupported`, `ErrDegenerate`) is skipped and never a wrong answer. The seeds are fixed, so a failure repeats. The same shapes scaled to a cube side of 0.1, 0.3, one third and 0.7, whose multiples are not whole numbers, are run through `Combine`, `Offset` and `MinWall`: each either answers inside 1e-9 of the cube count times the cube's volume or declines, and a tolerance wide enough to read two neighbouring coordinates as one fails it. A cut that keeps the common part, a dilation that tests one cube too few, and a concave edge read as one solid cube of four each fail them. |
| B16 | The document's operations are checked against the same cube by cube oracle as B15 (`apitest/orthogonal_differential_test.go`): random solids of a 5 x 5 x 5 grid of unit cubes are read in with `SolidFromMesh` and run through `Union`, `Cut`, `Intersect`, `Shell` with no opening and `Offset` both ways, each volume inside its stated bound of the cubes' count (the set less its erosion for the shell, the dilation or the erosion for the offsets), and through `Verify` for the thinnest wall, the shortest run of solid cubes along any line of cubes, and a pull along +z, blocked exactly when some column of cubes holds a solid, an empty and a solid cube again, `Union`, `Cut` and `Intersect` of a mesh solid with a rectangular prism on integer coordinates at a random height (B7's mixed route, eight in ten answering with a zero bound), `Stretch` by factors of 2, 3, one half, -1 and -2 about a random anchor (the volume times the factors' product in size, and the centre of each of forty random cubes carried to its stretched place, inside the result when the cube was solid), `MirroredCopy` across a plane through a whole number of cubes and its `Union` with the original (the cubes of the set and of its mirror image), `Patterned` as a circular pattern of two or four about a random centre (the cubes covered by the set and its turned copies), and `QuarterTurn` about a random point, whose volume is kept exactly and whose every cube centre, turned a quarter turn at a time, is inside the result when its cube is solid and outside when it is not. A set that is no solid is skipped and a result the document refuses is never a wrong volume. Nine pairs in ten answer on the grid with a zero bound, and the rest, whose kept cubes meet only along an edge, take the mesh route and stay inside its bound. The seeds are fixed. |
| B17 | `Chamfer` of several convex edges of a faceted solid that meets B1, which share corners and so have no route of their own (the retained blend route refuses "two selected edges share a corner"), is the solid less the wedge of each edge (`chamfer_wedges.go`): the wedge of a convex edge between faces with outward normals `n1` and `n2` is the triangle `{s ≤ 0, t ≤ 0, s + t ≥ −d}` in the plane square to the edge, swept along it, so the result at a corner where two or three selected edges meet is the solid within every chamfer plane, as a CAD chamfer of several edges is. Each wedge is cut by a prism whose triangle has legs `d + m` and whose margin `m` is the same behind both faces, so its hypotenuse is exactly the chamfer plane, and which reaches `e0` and `e1` before and past the edge's ends, `m`, `e0` and `e1` from half of `d` to `d` and distinct across the selection so that no face of one tool lies in a face of another or of the solid. The edge qualifies when it is straight along a world axis between two faces square to it and to each other, and the solid holds, in the tool's box, only the quadrant behind both faces (nothing beyond the faces or past the ends, which would be cut with the wedge) and the `d` by `d` block behind both faces along the whole edge, both read on the grid with `OverlapVolume`. At most 32 edges, no tangent chain and no asymmetric setback; otherwise the original refusal stands. The result is the mesh Cuts', a faceted body with a bound. The tests chamfer the eight 20 mm edges of a 20 x 20 x 10 box by 1 mm (4000 − (80 − 8/3)) and all twelve (4000 − 94, the corner cube keeping a quarter), read points either side of the chamfer plane, and refuse a setback deeper than the box and the two top edges that meet at the inside corner of an L. A margin that differs between the two faces tilts the chamfer plane and fails the volume, and a skipped fit check fails both refusals. |
| B18 | `Chamfer` of several concave edges of a faceted solid that meets B1, whose ends are in material or open to air, is the solid plus the wedge of each edge (`chamfer_wedges.go`): a concave edge's wedge is the triangle `{s ≥ 0, t ≥ 0, s + t ≤ d}` of void in front of both faces, and the tool is the triangle `{−m, −m}, {d + m, −m}, {−m, d + m}` swept from `e0` before the edge's start to `e1` past its end and united, so its hypotenuse is the chamfer plane and the rest of it is inside the walls. The fit check reads three boxes on the grid with `OverlapVolume`: the tool's box holds exactly what is behind a face (the box less the `(d + m)`-square of void along the edge), and each extension past an end is wholly solid, and the tool runs on into it, or wholly air, as past a pocket's mouth or a groove's open end, and the tool stops flush with the end; the Union of a flush end is the shifted retry's contact (`agent-docs/gap-tolerance-design.md` §5.2), so the tools that run on into material go first, exact, and the flush ones last, because each shifted Boolean is charged its shift and a chain of them passes the chord tolerance. An extension that is part solid and part air is declined. A selection is all convex or all concave. The tests chamfer the four 10 mm floor edges of a 10 x 10 x 5 pocket in a 30 x 30 x 10 slab (8500 + 20 − 4/3, two wedges sharing `d³/3` at each floor corner), read points either side of the chamfer plane, and the eight edges of the pocket, whose four wall corners stop flush at the mouth (8500 + 30 − 3 within a bound under 1 mm³), a groove cut through a slab whose floor edges run flush out of both ends (7530), and refuse a 6 mm fill up 5 mm walls. Refusing a flush end fails the pocket test, and running the flush tools first fails it too. |
| B19 | `Extrude` to a `ToFace` stop that the level route refuses because the face is tilted from the sweep (`extrude_tilted_face.go`) is the extrusion cut at the face's plane: the profile is extruded a distance past the farthest vertex of the face, and the cut tool is the prism standing on the face's own outline and running to twice that distance along the sweep. The face qualifies when its body has exact vertices (no vertex bound), its outline is one loop of exactly coplanar vertices ordered convex (read in rational arithmetic), all of it lies strictly on one side of the sketch plane, its normal is at least 1e-3 off perpendicular to the sweep, and the extrusion's bounding box, seen along the sweep, lies inside the outline by its bound plus 1e-6 of its size. The tool's bottom is the face's own vertices, so the cut plane is the face's plane and no plane rounding is charged; the tool's walls fall outside the extrusion by the margin. The result is a mesh Cut, a faceted body with a bound; any step the route cannot do leaves the original refusal and retires the half-built extrusion. An offset, a taper and a surface result keep the refusal. The tests extrude a rectangle up to a 10 mm ramp of a mesh block (volume 540), sweep one down onto it (6 × (200 − 90)), and refuse a footprint that runs off the ramp, where skipping the coverage check fails. |
| B20 | `Hole` in a planar face of a faceted solid with exact vertices (`hole_faceted_site.go`): the face is read as one convex plane polygon in rational arithmetic (the reading B19 uses), its unit normal rounded once to a float is the hole's axis, as an analytic plane's frame normal is, and the tool starts outside the body so the mouth plane is not the face's. The outward side is the one on which a point `4 × 0.05` mm out from the face's area centroid (B-centroid, mesh-solid §7) is certified outside the body and the point the same distance in is inside; any other answer is `ErrUnsupported`. The rest is the existing hole: plan, tools, one chained cut. A face with several loops, a non-convex or non-exact one, and a body with a vertex bound keep the refusal. `Boss` and `Emboss` read the same site, so a boss and a relief stand on the face along its normal. The tests bore 4 mm by 3 mm blind into the slope of a ramp tilted from every axis (π·4·3 removed within the bounds, void along the normal), stand a boss of the same size on it (π·4·3 added, solid along the normal), and raise and engrave a 4 x 4 relief 2 mm on it (±32); replacing the normal by z fails all four. |
| B21 | `Draft` of a faceted solid that is a straight prism along a world axis (`draft_mesh_prism.go`) is the tapered `Extrude` of its section, which is what a draft of every wall of a prism is (draft design §10): the body has exact vertices and two distinct coordinates along the axis, `NeutralFace` is a retained plane square to the axis at one of them, the selection is exactly the faces that are not the two caps, and the section at mid height, read exactly on the grid with `orthoshell.SectionLoops`, is one loop without a hole. The section is sketched on the neutral cap's plane with its normal toward the other cap and swept the body's height with `WithTaper(angle)`, so a positive angle narrows the body away from the neutral cap as a prism draft does, and the input body is retired. The tests draft a 20 x 10 x 8 block 5 degrees about its bottom (volume 200·8 − 30k·64 + 4k²·512/3 with k = tan 5°, the corner points either side of the leaning walls) and about its top (the same volume, still occupying z 0 to 8), and refuse two walls, a cap among the walls and a stepped body; dropping the taper fails the first two, dropping the selection check fails the third and sweeping always up fails the second. |

### 2. Not covered

- A mixed pair whose second operand tessellates with a bound or a slanted triangle.
- A pair with a slanted or curved face, or an operand with a vertex bound (the result of a general mesh Boolean, or a body placed by a motion whose vertex images are not floats).
- A many-operand `Union` whose operands are not all faceted solids that meet B1 or B7: B11 takes three or more of that kind in one step, and any other set folds two at a time through the pair route when its pairs qualify.

## Section

`Body.Section` of a faceted solid every face of which squares to a world axis is read from the grid of the
solid's coordinates (`internal/orthoshell/section.go`, hook `section_orthogonal.go`), so a mesh solid, a result of
the orthogonal Boolean (`agent-docs/orthogonal-design.md`) or of `MoveFace`, `Stretch` and the orthogonal
`Shell` sections to a sketch that enters a feature, with exact corners.

### 1. Contract

| Decision | Rule |
|---|---|
| X1 | The route applies to a faceted body with no vertex bound and every triangle exactly square to an axis, cut by a plane whose `U`, `V` and normal are exactly world axes (either sign, any pairing). Any other body or plane takes the other routes, and the route declines to them on every refusal below. |
| X2 | The plane lies strictly inside one layer of the grid along its normal, farther than the grid tolerance from every grid line, so the cut reads the same just either side of it. A plane on a grid line, outside the solid, or through a layer with no cell declines. |
| X3 | The cut is the boundary of the union of that layer's occupied cells, traced with collinear corners merged: every corner is a coordinate of the solid, an outer loop runs counter-clockwise round material and a hole clockwise. Cells that meet only at a corner decline. |
| X4 | The loops are stated in the plane's frame as `u = ±(p − origin)` along `U` and likewise `v`; a corner whose frame coordinate does not give back the world coordinate exactly (`origin + u = p`) declines, and a frame that flips handedness reverses its loops to keep their sense. |
| X5 | Every region of the cut is a profile of the one returned sketch, an outer loop and the holes inside it, in the order the outer loops are traced: a hole belongs to the smallest outer loop that contains its first corner, so a post standing in a frame's hollow is a profile of its own beside the frame's wall. The sketch is rebuilt by `sectionclip.ReconstructExactSections`, which checks that Sketch arranges every record as exactly its region (the empty space a hole encloses, which Sketch also arranges, is left out), so each profile measures exactly. |
| X6 | The tests section an L-shaped union in plan, with the plane axes swapped and with one reversed, and in two vertical cuts, read the exact areas, extrude a section to the matching volume, section a plate with a bored hole to a profile with a hole, list the two blocks a slot leaves and a post inside a frame beside the frame, and refuse a plane on a face or inner line, one past the solid and one oblique. A reversed outer-loop test, a skipped handedness flip or a hole given to the wrong outer loop fails them. |

### 2. Not covered

- A body with a vertex bound, such as one placed by a motion whose vertex images are not floats, and a plane that is not square to an axis.

## Offset

`Offset` of a faceted solid every face of which squares to a world axis moves every face along its outward normal by a
stated distance, neighbouring faces extended or trimmed to meet, and returns the result as a second solid. It is the
grid code of the orthogonal shell (`agent-docs/orthogonal-design.md`) in `internal/orthoshell/offset.go`; the
root entry is `offsetSolid` in `offset.go`.

### 1. Contract

| Decision | Rule |
|---|---|
| F1 | The receiver is a live faceted solid whose held vertices carry a zero bound and every triangle of which squares to an axis exactly (a mesh solid, a result of the orthogonal Boolean, `MoveFace`, `Stretch` or an orthogonal `Shell`). Any other solid keeps `ErrUnsupported` ("Offset requires a sheet body ..."); a sheet takes the route of `agent-docs/surface-design.md` §17. |
| F2 | `distance` is a positive length, as for a sheet. `OffsetPositive` (the default) moves each face outward, growing the solid, and `OffsetNegative` inward, shrinking it. A zero distance is `ErrDegenerate`, a wrong kind `ErrUnitKind` and a negative value `ErrNegativeMagnitude`. |
| F3 | The result is the solid dilated (or eroded) by the cube of half-side `distance`, computed on the grid of the solid's coordinates and those coordinates `± distance`. For faces that all square to an axis that is exactly the mitred face offset: a convex edge stays sharp and a reflex edge fills, a slot narrower than twice the distance closes, and a part thinner than twice it is gone. The new coordinates are the float sums `x ± distance`, to the rounding of the sum, as for `Shell`. |
| F4 | An erosion that leaves no cell is `ErrDegenerate`. A result the solid proof refuses (kept cells that meet only along an edge) is `ErrUnsupported`. A refusal registers nothing. |
| F5 | The receiver stays live: the result is committed as one `OperationOffset` with the receiver referenced and not consumed, as for a sheet. |
| F6 | The tests grow and shrink a block and read bounds and volumes, offset an L-shaped union both ways (the sharp reflex corner checked by point), close a 1 mm slot in a plate by growing it, refuse to shrink a 2 mm sheet by 1 mm, and refuse a non-faceted solid, a zero distance, a wrong kind and a negative value. Dropping the solid's own cells from a growth fails them. |

### 2. Not covered

- A solid with a slanted or curved face, a rounded offset (a radius at convex edges), and a vertex bound.
- An offset of selected faces, which `MoveFace` does one face at a time.

## Move

`MoveFace` is the press-pull of a CAD system for a faceted solid whose every face squares to a world axis, such as a part read from STL
or STEP and made a solid by `SolidFromMesh` (`agent-docs/mesh-solid-design.md`). It moves one planar face along its normal and lets
the walls beside it follow. The construction is the grid code of the orthogonal shell (`agent-docs/orthogonal-design.md`),
in `internal/orthoshell/press.go`; the root entry is `move_face.go`.

### 1. Contract

```go
func MoveFace(ctx context.Context, b *Body, sel FaceSelector, distance units.Value) (*Body, error)
```

| Decision | Rule |
|---|---|
| M1 | `b` MUST be a live faceted solid whose held vertices all carry a zero bound and every triangle of which squares to a world axis exactly, otherwise `ErrUnsupported`; any other receiver keeps no route here. `sel` MUST resolve to exactly one face (`ErrDegenerate`, as a nil selector is `errNilSelector`). A nil context or body is `ErrDegenerate`. |
| M2 | `distance` is a signed length. A positive distance moves the face outward along its outward normal, growing the body, and a negative one inward, cutting it. Zero is `ErrDegenerate`, a non-length `ErrUnitKind`, and a non-finite value `ErrNotFinite`. |
| M3 | The face's triangles lie on one plane with one outward direction, and the grid is the solid's coordinates plus the plane moved to, `P + sign·distance`, a float sum. The face's footprint is its cells where its triangles cover more than half. |
| M4 | The result is the body joined to the prism of cells the face sweeps over its footprint (outward) or less it (inward). A prism that would enter material outward, or leave the solid inward (out through the opposite wall, or past a notch), or a move that leaves no solid, is `ErrDegenerate`: the face would pass through another part. |
| M5 | The mesh of the result's boundary is built as the shell's walls are, and read back by `solidFromMeshBody`, so coplanar triangles form faces by exact plane and every axis face keeps its plane. The result is committed as one `OperationMoveFace` entry with `b` consumed; a refusal leaves the document unchanged. |
| M6 | Moving a face keeps its neighbours' planes where the body is a polyhedron of axis faces: an outer wall moved outward lengthens the walls beside it, and a notch bottom moved in widens the notch's floor, because the swept prism is exactly the volume the face's motion adds or removes. |

### 2. Not covered

- A solid with a slanted or curved face, one with a vertex bound (a Boolean result), and a body that is not faceted.
- Several faces in one call: call `MoveFace` per face.
- Rotating a face, tapering the move, and a move that merges, splits or removes a face: delete-and-heal is not here.

## Wall survey

`Verify` with `WithMinWallThickness` reads the thinnest wall of a faceted solid every face of which squares to a world
axis exactly, from the grid of its coordinates (`internal/orthoshell/wall.go`, called by
`internal/verifyrun/faceted_wall.go`). The ray survey of `agent-docs/mesh-wall-survey-design.md` can prove a wall too
thin and never thick enough; this reading proves both. The undercut survey of such a solid against a pull along a world axis is read from the same grid (§3).

### 1. Contract

| Decision | Rule |
|---|---|
| E1 | The reading applies to a faceted solid whose held vertices all carry a zero bound and every triangle of which squares to an axis exactly (a mesh solid, a result of the orthogonal Boolean, `MoveFace`, `Stretch`, `Offset` or an orthogonal `Shell`). Any other faceted body takes the rays. |
| E2 | The thinnest wall is the shortest run of material along any of the three axes: over every line of the solid's grid parallel to an axis, each maximal stretch of occupied cells is a run, and its length is the sum of the cell gaps. For faces that all square to an axis that is the least distance between two opposite faces whose footprints overlap, which is what a wall is. A fin, a neck or a floor is a run, wherever the rays' centre samples fall. |
| E3 | The reading is that length with a bound of `1e-12` of the larger of 1 mm and the solid's extent, for the rounding of the sums. Its interval has a lower end as well as an upper one, so an interval wholly above the tool is `Sound` (no wall finding), one wholly below is `DiagWallTooThin`, `Violating`, and one that reaches the tool is `DiagUndecidedWall`, `Suspect`. A solid with no material is not read this way. |
| E4 | The tests read an L whose legs are 4 mm thick against 2, 4 and 5 mm tools (`Sound` with no finding, undecided, proven too thin with the exact 4 mm), a cup with 1 mm walls against 2 and 0.5 mm tools and a thick block with a 0.5 mm fin. A survey that falls back to the rays leaves the thick-enough case undecided and fails them. |

### 3. Undercuts against an axis pull

| Decision | Rule |
|---|---|
| E5 | For a pull exactly along a world axis, `±X`, `±Y` or `±Z`, on a body that meets E1, a face square to the pull is released along its own outward normal and is blocked when material lies beyond it, in that direction, on any line over any grid cell it covers with positive area (`orthoshell.Undercuts`). A face square to the pull's axis is tested; one square to another axis is edge on and is never blocked, as U1 of `agent-docs/mesh-wall-survey-design.md`. |
| E6 | The test is made on every line over every covered cell, so the survey decides each face: the blocked faces are listed and are `DiagUndercut`, `Violating`, and a body with none has a proven all-clear, an empty listing with no finding and no undecided mark, so a part free to pull can be `Sound`. A pull that is not exactly an axis, or a body outside E1, takes the rays and their refusal to prove a pull free. |
| E7 | The tests pull a block along four axis directions to `Sound`, pull a T-shaped part with a cap along z (the cap's underside and the base's top are the two blocked faces) and free along x and y, and find a closed cavity blocked. A survey that falls back to the rays leaves the free pulls undecided and fails them. |

### 4. Concave radius

| Decision | Rule |
|---|---|
| E8 | On a body that meets E1 every edge is sharp, so a concave edge is a concave feature of radius zero. A concave edge is a grid line with three of the four cells around it occupied (`orthoshell.HasConcaveEdge`). A body with one reads `ScalarMeasured` with an exact zero minimum, and a body with none has no concave feature, `ScalarAbsent`, proven. |
| E9 | The tests read a block (`ScalarAbsent`, `Sound`), an L-shaped union and a shelled cup (`ScalarMeasured`, zero with a zero bound). A survey that falls back to the staged refusal fails them. A slanted faceted body keeps the staged `DiagUnsupportedSurveyPayload`. |

### 2. Not covered

- A faceted body with a slanted or curved face, or with a vertex bound, which keeps the ray survey and its refusal to prove a wall thick enough.
- A pull that is not along a world axis, and the concave-radius survey of a faceted body that is not axis-square.
